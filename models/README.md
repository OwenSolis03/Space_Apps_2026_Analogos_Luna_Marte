# `models/` — Scientific Methodology & Mission Similarity Index (MSI)

This directory documents the mathematical models, algorithmic formulations, and statistical frameworks powering **PROTOTERRA**.

In alignment with aerospace engineering rigor and open-science principles, PROTOTERRA rejects unexplainable "black-box" deep learning models in favor of a **fully auditable, deterministic, and physically grounded similarity engine**.

---

## The Philosophy of Explainable Similarity

When selecting a terrestrial test site for an Artemis lunar lander or a Mars rover, aerospace engineers must understand *why* an analog site was recommended and *in which exact physical regimes* the comparison remains valid.

Every similarity score generated by PROTOTERRA is decomposed into an explicit vector of physical dimensions and accompanied by a **"Where the Analogy Breaks"** warning report.

---

## Mathematical Formulation

### 1. Standardized 32 km Local AEQD Patches

To eliminate high-latitude polar distortion inherent in cylindrical map projections (crucial for Artemis III landing zones near the lunar South Pole), both planetary and terrestrial digital elevation models are reprojected into local **Azimuthal Equidistant (AEQD)** coordinate systems centered on each patch:

$$x = R \cdot \theta \cdot \sin(\Delta \lambda), \quad y = R \cdot (\cos\phi_0 \sin\phi - \sin\phi_0 \cos\phi \cos(\Delta \lambda))$$

Each patch spans exactly **$32\text{ km} \times 32\text{ km}$**, capturing both regional landscape context and mesoscale geomorphology.

---

### 2. Scale-Invariant Morphometric Feature Vector

For each 32 km patch, a morphometric feature vector $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^5$ is extracted:

$$\mathbf{x} = \Big[ \bar{z},\ \Delta z,\ \bar{s},\ s_{90},\ \text{TRI} \Big]$$

- **$\bar{z}$ (Mean Elevation):** Central topographic baseline.
- **$\Delta z$ (Topographic Relief):** $z_{\max} - z_{\min}$ within the 32 km radius.
- **$\bar{s}$ (Mean Slope):** Average slope calculated from finite difference gradients:
  $$s = \arctan\sqrt{\left(\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\partial z}{\partial y}\right)^2}$$
- **$s_{90}$ (90th Percentile Slope):** Robust measure of extreme terrain hazards and escarpments.
- **$\text{TRI}$ (Terrain Ruggedness Index):** Mean elevation difference between each cell and its 8 neighboring cells:
  $$\text{TRI} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i,j} \sqrt{\frac{1}{8}\sum_{k \in \mathcal{N}_8} (z_k - z_{ij})^2}$$

---

### 3. Dimension-Specific Similarity via Gaussian RBF Kernels

For each physical dimension $d$, the normalized feature distance $D_d$ between a candidate Earth patch $E$ and a target planetary site $T$ is transformed into a continuous similarity score $S_d \in [0, 1]$ using a **Radial Basis Function (RBF) Gaussian kernel**:

$$S_d(E, T) = \exp\left(-\frac{D_d(E, T)^2}{2\sigma_d^2}\right)$$

Where $\sigma_d$ represents the characteristic tolerance bandwidth for dimension $d$, empirically calibrated against known analog stations.

#### The 7 Physical Similarity Dimensions:
1. **$S_{\text{topo}}$ (Topography):** RBF distance across normalized relief $\Delta z$ and elevation distributions.
2. **$S_{\text{rough}}$ (Roughness):** RBF distance across $\bar{s}$, $s_{90}$, and Terrain Ruggedness Index ($\text{TRI}$).
3. **$S_{\text{env}}$ (Environment):** Ambient surface environmental and gravity/atmospheric context.
4. **$S_{\text{therm}}$ (Thermal):** Comparison of annual and diurnal surface temperature ranges (MODIS LST vs. planetary thermal models).
5. **$S_{\text{geo}}$ (Geology):** Categorical compatibility matrix bridging GLiM lithologies with lunar/Martian USGS stratigraphic units.
6. **$S_{\text{dust}}$ (Dust):** Climatological aerosol optical depth (MODIS MOD08_M3 AOD vs. Martian CDOD).
7. **$S_{\text{ice}}$ (Ice):** Ice stability indicators (Permanently Shadowed Regions on Moon, subsurface permafrost on Mars).

*(Note: Logistical accessibility metrics, such as distance to paved roads and commercial airports, are computed as operational layers, not similarity dimensions).*

---

### 4. Mission Similarity Index (MSI)

The overall composite score is computed as a weighted harmonic-arithmetic blend tailored to specific mission profiles:

$$\text{MSI}(E, T; \mathbf{w}) = \frac{\sum_{d=1}^7 w_d \cdot S_d(E, T)}{\sum_{d=1}^7 w_d}$$

Where $\mathbf{w} = [w_1, \dots, w_7]$ represents the mission weight vector.

## Spatial Diversity & Candidate Dispersion

To prevent candidate clustering (e.g., hundreds of adjacent tiles in the same Sahara erg dominating the top rankings), PROTOTERRA enforces a strict **$150\text{ km}$ minimum Haversine separation constraint**:

$$d_{\text{haversine}}(E_i, E_j) = 2R \arcsin\sqrt{\sin^2\left(\frac{\Delta \phi}{2}\right) + \cos\phi_i \cos\phi_j \sin^2\left(\frac{\Delta \lambda}{2}\right)} \ge 150\text{ km}$$

When candidate $E_i$ is accepted into the top-$N$ list for target $T$, all subsequent candidates within $150\text{ km}$ are suppressed, guaranteeing geographic diversity across continents.

---

## "Where the Analogy Breaks"

For every matched pair, PROTOTERRA calculates a **mismatch deviation vector**:

$$\mathbf{\delta}(E, T) = \frac{\mathbf{x}_E - \mathbf{x}_T}{\mathbf{\sigma}_T}$$

Any dimension where $|\delta_d| > 1.5\sigma$ is flagged with an explicit warning banner in the user interface, along with permanent physical planetary warnings (e.g., $g_{\text{Moon}} = 0.166g$, hard vacuum, solar particle events).
